## Intent

**Problem:** Since `11d`, `transcript drop` reads a Teams `.docx` export, but it still asks for the meeting's title, start, attendees and duration, and without a terminal it refuses when they are not given as options. The export itself holds most of this: the lines before the first turn give a title, a date and time, and a length; the turns give who spoke and roughly how long the meeting ran; the file has a creation and a modification time; and a calendar record of the same meeting may already be stored. Andrei hit this on his first real drop (2026-10-21). His direction of 2026-10-21: asking the owner is too expensive, so pm-ai extracts everything it can and asks nothing. `11d`'s review recorded the gap (`deferred-work.md`, "Surfaced by the story-11d review").

**Approach:** Every field is decided from the sources pm-ai already holds, in a fixed order per field, and the accuracy required follows what the field is for. For a past meeting, what was said and who was there are critical; the title matters; the exact start and the length do not, so an estimate is used and labelled as one. The speakers' display names become the meeting's participants, by name. A header no fixed reader understands is read by the local model, which sees only those cleaned header lines. Nothing is asked and nothing is refused for missing details, with or without a terminal. The closing line, and `--dry-run` before it, say each value, where it came from, and whether it is estimated. Options (`--title`, `--start`, `--duration`, `--attendees`) still win over the file. Everything else is `11d`'s and `11c`'s: readers, capture, ingestion, the daemon never asks (AD-21). Amends `11c` and `11d` (every question and every refusal for a missing title, start, attendees or duration is withdrawn), `11b`'s binding and adoption for an estimated start, and `11d`'s Never on `.docx` document properties for `dcterms:created` alone.

## Boundaries & Constraints

**Always:**

- **The fields, by criticality.** For each field, the first source in order that gives a value decides; a source that gives nothing, or a value the field's rule refuses, passes to the next.

  | Field | Criticality | Sources, in order | When nothing decides |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Content | critical | the transcript body, read by `11d`'s readers | no change from `11d` (a file no reader accepts is refused, as today) |
  | Participants | critical | the speakers' display names, as written | never empty when anything was said: every turn has a speaker |
  | Title | medium | `--title`; the header's title line with the recording stamp removed; the local model's reading of the header; the file name without the stamp and the extension — but when the fixed readers leave the start or the length unread (a layout they do not know), the model's validated title comes before the header's title line | the file name always gives one |
  | Start | low, an estimate is acceptable | 1. `--start`; 2. a matching calendar-sourced meeting; 3. the `.docx` creation time minus the length; 4. the header's date and time read in `display_timezone`, or in the machine's zone when it is unset; 5. the file's modification time minus the length | step 5 always gives one |
  | Length | low | `--duration`; the header's length line; a WebVTT file's last cue end; the local model's reading of the header; the last turn's start time | stored as unknown |

- **The header.** Only a file read by `11d`'s speaker-and-time reader has one: its non-blank lines before the first turn (`11d`'s definition), whether the text came from a `.docx` or is a `.md`. WebVTT and `Speaker: words` files have none; their title comes from `--title` or the file name, and their start from the steps that do not need a header. Header lines are never utterances (unchanged).
- **How a header line is prepared before it is judged.** Each line is trimmed; every Unicode space character (the no-break space U+00A0, the narrow no-break space U+202F, and any other character Unicode counts as a space) becomes an ordinary space, and runs of spaces become one. Markdown's marks are set aside: those `_unmarked` sets aside on a speaker line (a quotation's `>`s, a heading's `#`s, a list marker, a trailing backslash or two trailing spaces), `*` or `_` emphasis wrapping the whole line (`**…**`, `*…*`, `__…__`, `_…_`), and a backslash before a punctuation character (`\_` read as `_`). A line that is then only the word `Transcript`, in any case, is skipped.
- **The fixed readers: two date forms, measured on two real exports.** A start line is exactly one of:
  - day first: a day of one or two digits, a space, an English month name in full, a space, a four-digit year, a comma, optional spaces, then the time (`14 September 2026, 09:30am`);
  - month first: an English month name in full, a space, a day of one or two digits, a comma, a space, a four-digit year, a comma, optional spaces, then the time (`September 24, 2026, 1:17PM`).

  The time is an hour of one or two digits, a colon, two digits of minutes, an optional space, then `am` or `pm` in any case. Month names are matched ignoring case. The 12-hour rules: `12am` is 00, `12pm` is 12, hours 1 to 11 `pm` add 12; an hour of 0 or over 12, or minutes over 59, is not a start. The date must be a real calendar date (`31 September 2026` is not). Any other layout is not read by the fixed readers and goes to the local model.
- **The length line.** Exactly one of these forms, with or without a space between the parts: `Nm NNs` (`29m 03s`, `41m 15s`, both measured), `Nh NNm NNs`, `Nm`, `Nh NNm` (the last three unmeasured, covered by tests only). Seconds are under 60, and minutes after an hour are under 60. Any seconds round the length up to the next whole minute, as a last cue does (`29m 03s` is 30, `29m 00s` is 29).
- **Which header line gives which field.** A line is *date-like* when the whole line is a date or a time: it holds a time of day (digits, a colon, two digits, with or without `am` or `pm`), or a four-digit year from 1900 to 2099 next to a month name or joined to other numbers by `-`, `/` or `.`, and nothing else — every word in it is a month or weekday name, `am`, `pm`, `at`, `UTC` or `GMT`, and every other character a digit, a space or one of `, . / : -`. A title that merely holds a year or a time (`Q3 2026 planning`, `Standup 10:00`) stays a title. A line is *length-like* when it is made only of numbers each followed by `h`, `m` or `s`, or is a time of three fields (`1:02:03`). The fixed readers read the start only when exactly one header line is date-like and it is in a start form, and the length only when exactly one header line is length-like and it is in a length form. The title is the first header line, and only when that line is neither date-like nor length-like; a later line is never taken as the title. So two start lines, two length lines, or one readable and one unreadable date-like line leave that field unread by the fixed readers; the other fields are still read. A title written across several lines is a recorded limit: the first line is the title.
- **The recording stamp.** When the title line ends with Teams' recording stamp, `-YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS-Meeting Recording`, that ending is removed (`Example Weekly Sync-up-20260914_093000-Meeting Recording` gives `Example Weekly Sync-up`). A title line holding `-YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS-` followed by other words (a stamp in another language) is unread by the fixed readers. The stamp's date and time are never used as the start: measured on one export, the stamp's time was three hours off the header's, so its zone is unknown.
- **A value is used only if the field's rule accepts it.** The title only when `_title_refusal`'s rule accepts it (so a title empty after the stamp is removed is unread); the length only within `11c`'s 1 to 1,440 minutes; the start only as a real date and time. A refused value is treated exactly as if the source had not given it.
- **The local model reads a header the fixed readers miss.** It is called only when the file has a header and the fixed readers leave at least one of the title, the start's date and time, or the length unread. It sees only the header lines, at most 20 lines and 2,000 characters (chosen, not measured; a longer header is not sent), each passed through `sanitize` (`pm_ai/domain/sanitize.py:712`), the same step transcript text goes through; the transcript body is never sent. The call is `ModelPort.complete` with a new local-only task class `header_reading`, the flow `ScopeFlow.of((scope,), scope)` (the flow table's existing row "a scope bound for itself", `pm_ai/domain/model_calls.py:151`), and a shape: `{"title": string or null, "start": string or null, "length_minutes": integer or null}`. pm-ai validates the answer: the title must appear, as written, inside one prepared header line (so the model cannot invent one), and then passes the stamp rule and the title rule; the start must be `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM`, a real date and time, with no offset, not after the file's own times (its creation or modification time) and not more than 30 days before them under any real UTC offset, and is then treated as the header's date and time (step 4 and the same-day hint); the length must be within 1 to 1,440. The model's start and length are labelled estimated. A field that fails is unread; the others are used. The call runs on the daemon's model pool, in its one local slot (AD-19), never beside it. It fills the fields the fixed readers left unread; when they left the start or the length unread, its validated title also replaces theirs. When no local model is configured, the model refuses or times out (`NoLocalModel`, `LocalModelUnavailable`, `ModelTimedOut`, `PossiblyTruncated`, `OutputCapReached`, `ScopeFlowRefused`), or the answer fails the shape, those fields pass to the next source; nothing is refused and the drop goes on.
- **Participants are the speakers, by name.** Andrei's decision of 2026-10-21, replacing the earlier attendees answer (a). The speakers' display names, exactly as the reader gives them (WebVTT voice names, `Speaker:` names, speaker-and-time names), are recorded as the meeting's participants, once each (two spellings with the same `normalise()` key, `pm_ai/core/aliases.py:52`, are one participant, kept as first written), in order of first turn.
  - **Stored as a name, never as a handle.** A participant is stored in a new pm-ai region of the meeting record, `## Participants`, one name per line (`- ` and the name written by `render_value` with `quote_marks`), between the field block and `## Notes`. `MeetingRecord` gains `participants`, beside `summary`. `Meeting.attendees` stays handles only (`pm_ai/domain/identity.py:328`); a name never enters it, so a comma in a name is never a list separator.
  - **Linked only on an exact match, when read.** A participant is linked to a handle only through the saved alias table (`aliases.md`, AD-34), when the name's `normalise()` key has a row (`AliasTable.handle_for(name, meeting_id)`, `pm_ai/core/aliases.py:161`). The link is made each time the record is read, never written, so a correction in `aliases.md` applies to past meetings (AD-34: resolved when read). No other matching: no partial name, no Graph display name, no guess. An unmatched name stays a name.
  - **Written whichever way the meeting was bound.** The region is written for a new record, a joined record and a `--meeting` record alike, by its own writer under the record's claim, as `put_summary` writes `## Summary`; every `put` carries it over, so a calendar harvest that adopts or rewrites the record keeps it (AD-33's rule for pm-ai regions). A second drop onto the same record is refused as today (the meeting already has a capture), so the region is written once.
  - **What each reader of attendees does.** `meeting_records.py:554` and `:658` write and read `attendees` unchanged, handles only; `as_stored` (`:355`) is unchanged, because participants live on `MeetingRecord`, not `Meeting`. `--attendees` still adds handles to a new record's `attendees`. A new record's `attendee_count` is its participants plus each `--attendees` handle that no participant's name is linked to when the drop runs; so `Meeting.__post_init__`'s check (`meetings.py:191`, the count at least the named handles) holds, and `man_hour_cost` (`meetings.py:208`, no caller yet) now counts the speakers. A joined calendar record keeps the calendar's `attendees` and `attendee_count`. The Graph connector (`connectors/graph/__init__.py:1735`, `:1739`, `:1803`, `:2186`) and the dashboard's attendee count (`rendering.py:711`) read calendar meetings live and are unchanged. Who spoke for attribution stays `2o`'s rule over utterance handles; a participant name changes no attribution.
- **The start, step by step.** Each step below is tried in order; the first that gives a start decides.
  1. `--start`, with its own offset. Binding then uses `11b`'s rule unchanged (title and start within 15 minutes, `MATCH_TOLERANCE`), including its refusal for two matching records.
  2. **A matching calendar-sourced meeting.** A record in the scope with a calendar reference (`calendar_event_ref` set) matches when its title equals the drop's title after `normalised_title` (`meetings.py:39`: NFKC, case-folding, trimming, collapsing whitespace) and it falls on the *hint day*. The hint day is the header's date when the header gives one (the fixed readers' or the model's reading); otherwise the date, in `display_timezone` (UTC when unset), of the first estimate steps 3 and 5 give. A drop whose day comes only from the file's modification time — no header date, no creation time deciding, no `--start` — joins no existing meeting: it makes a new record, and the closing line says why. A record falls on the hint day when its start's date in UTC, or in `display_timezone` when set, is that day; both are accepted because the measured export's header time was UTC, not the owner's zone. Exactly one candidate: the drop joins it, and the start is the record's own (source `calendar`, not estimated). Two or more: no match; a new record is made, and the closing line says so, naming each candidate's id. A record pm-ai minted (`manual_only`) is not a candidate in this step.
  3. **The `.docx` creation time minus the length.** Read from the archive's `docProps/core.xml`, element `dcterms:created`, in UTC (Word's form). Measured on one export: created 5 minutes 18 seconds after the meeting's end (header 13:17, length 41m 15s, created 14:03:33Z). This step decides only when a length is in use and the creation time is consistent with the header: when the header gives a date and time, the creation time must be at or after the end that date and time give under some real UTC offset (−12:00 to +14:00), and no more than 15 minutes after it (the tolerance; the measured 5 minutes 18 seconds is inside it). The start is the creation time minus the length, truncated to the minute. With no header date, the creation time minus the length decides, with no further bound. With no `dcterms:created`, a value not in UTC, no length, a `.md`, or a creation time outside the tolerance (an export saved long after the meeting), this step does not decide.
  4. **The header's date and time read in `display_timezone`,** or, when it is unset, in the machine's own zone (source `file header, machine zone`). A wall-clock time that occurs twice in that zone takes the earlier instant, and one that does not occur takes the offset before the change; either way it is an estimate.
  5. **The file's modification time minus the length** (the modification time itself when the length is unknown), read from the same `stat` the drop already makes (`dispatch.py:3561`). For a `.md` conversion this is when it was converted, a recorded limit.

  A start from steps 3, 4 or 5 is labelled `estimated` with its source (`docx creation time`, `file header`, `file header, read by the local model`, either of those two with `, machine zone`, `file modification time`); one from step 1 or 2 is not. The start is never asked and never refused.
- **A new record with an estimated start.** Its record carries a new optional field, `start_estimated_from`, naming the step's source (`docx-created`, `header`, `model-header`, `header-machine-zone`, `model-header-machine-zone`, `file-modified`), written only when the start is estimated. A later calendar harvest adopts such a manual-only record by step 2's rule (same hint day, here the record's own start's date in UTC or `display_timezone`, plus the title, exactly one candidate) rather than by the 15-minute window, so an estimate 20 minutes off is still adopted; a record whose start came only from the file's modification time (`file-modified`) is never adopted; adoption replaces the start with the calendar's and clears the field (amends `11b`'s adoption in `MeetingRecords._put_calendar`, `meeting_records.py:931`).
- **The length.** `--duration` first, which now also wins over a WebVTT last cue. Then the header's length line, the recorded length of the whole meeting. Then a WebVTT file's last cue end (`last_cue_end_minutes`, `transcripts.py:202`), which also covers the last utterance. Then the model's reading of the header. Last, the last turn's start time, rounded up to the whole minute (`11d`'s speaker-and-time reader), which misses the last turn's own length: measured on the fixture, the last turn starts at 28:57 and the header says `29m 03s`, so the header gives 30 and the last turn would give 29. With none, the length is unknown: `Meeting.duration_minutes` becomes `int | None`, the record omits `duration_minutes` (accepted only beside `manual_only=true`), `man_hour_cost` returns `None`, and the closing line and `--dry-run` say "length unknown". Every other reader of `duration_minutes` reads calendar meetings only and is unchanged: the dashboard (`rendering.py:661`, `:720`, `:744`), day placement (`pipelines.py:2636`), transcript acquisition (`transcript_acquisition.py:192`, which skips a record with no calendar reference first), and Graph transcript fitting (`graph_transcripts.py:720`). No dashboard lists a minted record today, so nothing there shows the unknown length.
- **The title read from the file is trusted as a typed title is.** It is not passed through the prompt-injection filter, as a typed title is not (Andrei's decision of 2026-10-21). A title the model gives is accepted only when it is text already in a header line, so it is no less trusted.
- **Nothing is asked, anywhere.** No question for the title, start, attendees, duration or zone, at a terminal or not; no confirmation line; no refusal for a missing detail. `_drop_identify`, `_drop_describe`, `_ATTENDEES_QUESTION`, the `identify` and `describe` callbacks, and `UnboundTranscript`'s missing-details refusals are removed. Refusals that are not about missing details stay as today: the file, the scope, a named `--meeting` that does not exist, a meeting that already has a capture, and, with `--start`, two matching records.
- **At the prompt (AD-21).** A drop typed at `pm-ai>` runs in the daemon, which never asks; since the drop now asks nothing, the prompt asks nothing either and sends the words as typed, with a relative path made absolute as today (`repl.py:_absolute_paths`). `drop_would_ask` and `ask_drop_here` are retired for `transcript drop` (`repl.py:655`). The local model call happens in the daemon, so the drop's bound at the daemon (`entry.py:559`, `TRANSCRIPT_DROP_BOUND_SECONDS`, `pipelines.py:1320`) becomes the `git` question plus one model call's deadline (`SUMMARY_CALL_DEADLINE_SECONDS`, `pipelines.py:2740`); the drop was already a job, being over 5 seconds.
- **The closing line.** For a drop without `--meeting`, it keeps its sentence about the capture and what was staged, then names the outcome — joined the existing meeting `<title>` (`mtg_…`), found by title on the same day (or by `--start`); or a new meeting `mtg_…`, with the ids of two or more same-day candidates when that is why — and then each field: the title and its source; the start in UTC (and in `display_timezone` when set) with its source and, when estimated, the word `estimated`; the length and its source, or "length unknown"; the participants, each with its linked handle or "not linked". Each value's source travels with the value from where it was decided to the closing line, in `TranscriptDropped` (replacing `duration_from_last_cue`, `pipelines.py:1274`); it is never inferred afterwards. A drop with `--meeting` closes as today, plus the participants.
- **`--dry-run`, a preview for wrappers.** `transcript drop <file> --scope <scope> --dry-run` reads the file, the records and `aliases.md`, makes the model call when one is needed, and prints what a drop would store: each field's value, source and whether it is estimated, the participants and their links, and the outcome (join `mtg_…` with its title, new, or refused, naming why). It writes nothing, asks nothing, and exits 0 whenever it can report; a file or scope the drop refuses is refused as today (exit 3), and a usage error is exit 2. `--json` with `--dry-run` prints one JSON object: `title`, `start` and `duration_minutes`, each `{"value", "source", "estimated"}` (`start`'s value an ISO 8601 UTC instant; `duration_minutes`' value `null` and source `"unknown"` when unknown); `participants`, a list of `{"name", "handle"}` (`handle` `null` when not linked); `outcome`, one of `{"kind": "join", "meeting_id", "title"}`, `{"kind": "new", "candidates": [ids]}` (empty when no same-day candidate) or `{"kind": "refused", "reason"}`. `--json` without `--dry-run` is a usage error. Both are flags in `TABLE` (`flags=`), which `4u`'s completer offers. Typed at the prompt, a dry run is sent as typed and runs in the daemon under the same bound.
- **The same rule everywhere.** The header is read, and each field decided, by one I/O-free function in `pm_ai/core`, given the text, the file's times, the creation time, `display_timezone`, the candidate records, the alias table and the model's validated reading; the drop and the dry run call the same function, so the preview is what the drop stores.
- **The fixture names no one.** New tests use only the names and words `11d`'s fixture already uses (`Example Weekly Sync-up`, `Alex Morgan`, `Sam Rivera`, placeholder lines) and `example.com` handles. Header variants, a `.docx` with a `docProps/core.xml`, and file times are built in the test. No line, property or time from a real export is added; the measured figures above are recorded as structure only.

**Ask First:**
1. If the recorded fixture's header lines, read through `docx_text`, differ from the forms above (for example the title line carries more than the stamp), before writing the header rule.

**Never:** No question and no confirmation line, at a terminal or not. No refusal for a missing title, start, attendees or length. No transcript body, and no header line before `sanitize`, sent to any model; no header sent to a frontier model. No person guessed from a display name, and no name stored as a handle. No recording stamp read as the start. No zone assumed for the header's time beyond `display_timezone`, then the machine's own zone, labelled as such. No document property read from a `.docx` other than `dcterms:created` in `docProps/core.xml` — never `dc:creator`, `cp:lastModifiedBy`, the title or any other. No write of any kind in `--dry-run`. No question asked by the daemon. No change to the readers' choice, the capture name rule or the sanitization boundary. No real names, spoken words, document properties or file times in a fixture or test.

## Design Notes

**Measured on two real exports, structure only.** The date lines differ: one export is day first with lowercase `am`/`pm` (`14 September 2026, 09:30am`), the other month first with uppercase (`September 24, 2026, 1:17PM`). In the second, the header time was 13:17, the length line `41m 15s`, and `dcterms:created` 14:03:33Z: consistent with the header being UTC and the export created about 5 minutes after the meeting ended (13:58:15), not with the header in the owner's zone (UTC+2, which would put the start at 11:17Z and the creation nearly two hours after the end). So the header time is not assumed to be in `display_timezone`, the creation-time estimate comes before the header-in-zone estimate, and the header's date is the same-day hint, with both UTC and `display_timezone` dates accepted. The recording stamp at the end of the title was 16:17:29, three hours off the header, so in yet another zone; it is never the start. No content, name or title from either export is recorded.

**Why the order.** The start decides only matching and placement, so an estimate is acceptable, but matching must survive one: an estimated start can be tens of minutes off, so same day and title, with exactly one candidate, replace the 15-minute window, and the same rule lets a later calendar harvest adopt the record. Two candidates are left unmatched rather than guessed. Every file with a header is a speaker-and-time file, so it always has turn times, and with the length order as directed the header's length line is used only when the last turn's time is out of range; it reads a minute or so over the last turn's start, which is the error accepted for a low-criticality field. The model reads only the header because the header is what varies between layouts; the body stays with `11d`'s readers and never reaches a model, and the title it gives must be text already in the header. Participants are stored as names in a region of their own because `attendees` is a comma-separated list of handles and AD-34 forbids a name standing in for a handle; linking at read time through `aliases.md` is AD-34's own rule.
