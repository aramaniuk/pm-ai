## Intent

**Problem:** Since `11d`, `transcript drop` reads a Teams `.docx` export, but it still asks for the meeting's title, start, attendees and duration, although the export's first three lines hold the title, the start date and time, and the length. Andrei hit this on his first real drop (2026-10-21) and called it poor; his standing rule is that a command never asks for what its input already says. `11d`'s review recorded the gap (`deferred-work.md`, "Surfaced by the story-11d review": "A `.docx` export's header holds the start date and time and the meeting's length (`NNm NNs`), but the drop still asks for both").

**Approach:** The drop reads the title, start and length from the lines before the first turn of a file read by `11d`'s speaker-and-time reader — a Teams `.docx` and its `.md` conversion — and keeps reading the length from a WebVTT file's last cue, as today. The start has no UTC offset in the file, so its zone is worked out (from `display_timezone`, a calendar record, the `.docx`'s creation time, then the machine's zone) and asked only when none of those decides. In order, the drop reads the header, then matches an existing record, then stores. What the file says is used with no confirmation and no question; only what the file does not say, or says in a way pm-ai cannot read, is asked. The closing line says what was used and where each value came from, and whether the transcript joined an existing meeting or made a new one. `--title`, `--start` and `--duration` still win over the file. `--dry-run` shows all of this and writes nothing. Andrei's decisions of 2026-10-21. Everything else is `11d`'s and `11c`'s: readers, binding, capture, ingestion, the questions asked where the person is and never by the daemon (AD-21). Amends `11c`'s and `11d`'s "the duration asked for anything that is not WebVTT", `11d`'s attendee question, and `11d`'s Never "No reading … document properties from a `.docx`" for the one property `dcterms:created`; fixes half of `11d`'s deferred entry on `--duration` being dropped without a word for a WebVTT file.

## Boundaries & Constraints

**Always:**
- **Where the header is.** Only a file read by `11d`'s speaker-and-time reader has one: its non-blank lines before the first turn (`11d`'s definition), whether the text came from a `.docx` or is a `.md`. WebVTT and `Speaker: words` files have no header; for WebVTT the last cue still gives the length (`11c`), and for both the title and start are asked as today.
- **How a header line is prepared before it is judged.** Each line is trimmed; every Unicode space character (the no-break space U+00A0, the narrow no-break space U+202F, and any other character Unicode counts as a space) becomes an ordinary space, and runs of spaces become one. Then Markdown's marks are set aside: those `_unmarked` sets aside on a speaker line (a quotation's `>`s, a heading's `#`s, a list marker, a trailing backslash or two trailing spaces), and in addition `*` or `_` emphasis wrapping the whole line (`**…**`, `*…*`, `__…__`, `_…_`) and a backslash before a punctuation character (`\_` read as `_`). A line that is then only the word `Transcript`, in any case, is skipped as if it were not there.
- **The start line.** Exactly this form: a day of one or two digits, a space, an English month name written in full (any case), a space, a four-digit year, a comma, optional spaces, an hour of one or two digits, a colon, two digits of minutes, an optional space, then `am` or `pm` in any case. So `14 September 2026, 09:30am`, `14 September 2026, 9:30am` and `14 September 2026, 09:30 AM` are the same start. The 12-hour rules: `12am` is 00, `12pm` is 12, hours 1 to 11 `pm` add 12; an hour of 0 or over 12, or minutes over 59, with `am` or `pm`, is not a start. The date must be a real calendar date (`31 September 2026` is not). The form was measured on the 11d fixture; the variants (case, spaces, one-digit hour) are covered by tests only.
- **The length line.** Exactly one of these forms, with or without a space between the parts: `Nm NNs` (`29m 03s`, measured), `Nh NNm NNs` (`1h 02m 03s`), `Nm` (`45m`), `Nh NNm` (`1h 05m`). Seconds are under 60, and minutes after an hour are under 60; the forms other than `Nm NNs` are guesses at how Teams writes a meeting over an hour or a round length, unmeasured, covered by tests only. Any seconds round the length up to the next whole minute, as a last cue does (`29m 03s` is 30, `29m 00s` is 29, `0m 20s` is 1).
- **Which line gives which field.** A line is *date-like* when it holds a time of day (digits, a colon, two digits, with or without `am` or `pm`), or a four-digit year from 1900 to 2099 next to a month name or joined to other numbers by `-`, `/` or `.`. A line is *length-like* when it is made only of numbers each followed by `h`, `m` or `s`, or is a time of three fields (`1:02:03`). The start is read only when exactly one header line is date-like and it is in the start form; the length only when exactly one header line is length-like and it is in a length form. So two start lines, two length lines, or one readable and one unreadable date-like line leave that field unread; the other fields are still read. The title is the first header line, and only when that line is neither date-like nor length-like; when it is either, the title is unread, and a later line is never taken as the title instead. A title written across several lines is a recorded limit: the first line is the title.
- **The recording stamp.** When the title line ends with Teams' recording stamp, `-YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS-Meeting Recording` (fitted to the 11d fixture and seen in one more real export's structure, Andrei's of 2026-10-21, of which no content is recorded), that ending is removed: `Example Weekly Sync-up-20260914_093000-Meeting Recording` gives `Example Weekly Sync-up`. The stamp's date and time are never read as the start. A title line holding `-YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS-` followed by any other words (a stamp in another language) leaves the title unread. A title read this way is expected to be the title a calendar record of the same meeting carries, but this is not measured; where they differ, nothing matches and a new record is made.
- **A value read is used only if its question would accept it.** The title only when `_title_refusal` accepts it (so an empty title, after the stamp is removed, is unread); the length only within `11c`'s 1 to 1,440 minutes (so `0m 00s` and `25h 00m 00s` are unread); the start only as a real date and time. An unread field is treated exactly as if the file had not said it.
- **Localised dates and stamps are recorded limits.** A date in another language or another order (`September 14, 2026, 9:30 AM`, `14 września 2026, 09:30`, `14 Sep 2026, 09:30am`) is date-like and not in the start form, so the start is unread and asked — never read as a title.
- **The title read from the file is trusted as a typed title is.** It is not passed through the prompt-injection filter (`pm_ai/core/sanitize.py`), as a typed title is not. Andrei's decision of 2026-10-21: he does not want to confirm it, and the file is his own export.
- **The start's zone.** The start line carries no UTC offset. The first of these that decides gives it:
  1. `display_timezone`, when set: the start is that wall-clock time in that zone.
  2. A calendar record: when `display_timezone` is not set, every UTC offset that some zone in the machine's timezone database uses on that date is tried; an offset counts when, at the instant it gives, the scope holds a record with a calendar reference (`calendar_event_ref`, so not one pm-ai minted) that `11b`'s rule matches by title (the title in use) and start (within 15 minutes). Exactly one offset counting decides.
  3. The `.docx`'s creation time: Word stores it in UTC in the archive's `docProps/core.xml`, element `dcterms:created`. Over the same offsets, an offset counts when the start instant it gives is at or before the creation instant and the creation instant is no more than the meeting's length (the length in use) plus 15 minutes after it. Exactly one offset counting decides. With no length in use, no `dcterms:created`, a value not in UTC, or a `.md`, this step does not decide. Whether Teams writes this time at the meeting's end or when the export is downloaded is not measured; when it is later than the length plus 15 minutes, this step does not decide. The 15 minutes is chosen, not measured.
  4. The machine's local zone, as the operating system applies it to that date and time.
  Where a zone is named (step 1 or 4) and the wall-clock time occurs twice in it (the hour repeated when clocks go back) or not at all (the hour skipped when they go forward), that zone does not decide alone: steps 2 and 3 are applied to that zone's two offsets, before and after the change, and decide if exactly one counts. If nothing decides, the person is asked, at a terminal, only for what is missing, with the date and time read shown: for a repeated hour, which of the two instants (`1` for the first, with its offset, or `2` for the second); otherwise the zone or the offset (an IANA zone name from the machine's database, matched ignoring case, or `+HH:MM` / `-HH:MM` from `-12:00` to `+14:00`). The full start is never asked again. Without a terminal it is refused naming `--start <start>` and `display_timezone`, before anything is written (exit 3). A `--start` given as an option carries its own offset and none of this applies.
- **What is used, field by field, and in what order.** The drop reads the header, then matches an existing record, then stores. For the title and the start: an option given (`--title`, `--start`) first; else what the file says (with the zone worked out as above); else, at a terminal, the question `11c` asks; else, without a terminal, `11c`'s refusal naming the missing option, before anything is written. For the length, only when no record matches and a new one is made (as `11c` does): `--duration` first — which now also wins over a WebVTT file's last cue — then the file's header or last cue, then the question at a terminal, then the refusal without one. No confirmation line and no question about a value the file gave: Andrei's decision of 2026-10-21; he does not want to be involved in meeting details the transcript already carries.
- **Attendees.** Andrei's decision (a) of 2026-10-21. The file lists speakers by display name; attendees are person handles (AD-34), and pm-ai never turns a display name into a person by guessing. So attendees are never asked and never refused, with or without a terminal: a new record has none unless `--attendees` is given, and speakers stay what they are today, the utterances' speaker labels. With no attendees, a minted record is written with an empty `attendees` value and `attendee_count` 0, and its Man-Hour Cost is 0; the dashboard's meeting lines count attendees of calendar meetings read live, not of minted records.
- **Answers.** Each remaining question's answer is trimmed and judged ignoring case. A refused answer is asked again with the reason, as today. Ctrl-C or end of input at any remaining question writes nothing: "Nothing was written.", exit 3.
- **The closing line.** For a drop that bound by title and start, the closing line keeps its sentence about the capture and what was staged, and says what was used and where each value came from: the title and its source; the start with its offset, its source, and the zone's source (the option's own offset, `display_timezone`, a calendar record, the file's creation time, this machine's zone, or answered); then the outcome — joined the existing meeting `<title>` (`mtg_…`), or a new meeting `mtg_…` with its length and the length's source. The sources are `option`, `file header`, `last cue` and `answered`. Each value's source travels with the value from where it was decided to the closing line; it is never inferred afterwards (as `duration_from_last_cue` infers it today). A drop with `--meeting` closes as today.
- **`--dry-run`, a preview for wrappers.** `transcript drop <file> --scope <scope> --dry-run` reads the file and the records and prints what a drop would use — each field's value and source, the start's offset and the zone's source — and the outcome: join `mtg_…` (with its title), new (with the length and its source, or that the length would be asked), not known (the title or start would be asked), or that a drop would be refused (two records match, or the meeting already has a capture), naming why. It names each field a drop at a terminal would ask. It writes nothing, asks nothing even at a terminal, and exits 0 whenever it can report; a file or scope the drop refuses is refused as today (exit 3), and a usage error is exit 2. `--json` with `--dry-run` prints the same as one JSON object: `title`, `start` and `duration_minutes`, each `{"value", "source"}` (`null` value and source when unread); `zone` as `{"name", "offset", "source"}`; `outcome` as `{"kind": "join", "meeting_id", "title"}`, `{"kind": "new", "duration_minutes", "source"}`, `{"kind": "unknown"}` or `{"kind": "refused", "reason"}`; and `asks`, the fields that would be asked, in order. `--json` without `--dry-run` is a usage error. Both are flags in `TABLE` (`flags=`), which `4u`'s completer offers; a flag takes no value, so it needs no value source. Typed at the prompt, a dry run asks nothing and so is sent to the daemon as typed and runs there, under `transcript drop`'s existing bound (AD-21); `TERMINAL_BOUND` is unchanged.
- **At the prompt, where the person is (AD-21).** A drop typed at `pm-ai>` runs in the daemon, which never asks. So `drop_would_ask` is true for a drop without `--meeting` or `--dry-run`, missing any of `--title`, `--start` and `--duration`, at a terminal. Then `ask_drop_here`, in the prompt's process, reads the file's header with the same function the daemon uses, reads `config.toml` and its `display_timezone` with `load_config` (a file the loader refuses is refused with the loader's sentence, nothing sent, exit 3), applies step 1, and asks what is missing. When step 1 does not decide — `display_timezone` unset, or the time repeated or missing in its zone — step 2 needs the records, which the prompt's process cannot read, and comes before steps 3 and 4: so the prompt first sends the same words (with any title it asked) and `--dry-run --json` to the daemon, which applies every step, takes the zone and the outcome from its answer, and asks only what is still missing (the length only if the outcome is new). Otherwise the prompt does not know whether a record matches, and asks the length, when the file gives none, before the daemon knows (`11d`'s accepted limit, unchanged). The prompt always sends the resolved `--start` with its offset, so the daemon never interprets a header start itself, together with the title and length in use, and a `--sources` option naming each sent value's source (`title=file header,start=file header,zone=display_timezone,duration=file header`), so the closing line says the same as it would in a terminal. `--sources` is checked as the answers are: a source word not in the lists above, or a field not given with it, is a usage error, and it is refused with `--meeting`; given by hand, it changes only how the closing line words those values. It is free text for completion (`4u`'s rule).
- **A header pm-ai cannot read is not a refusal.** An unreadable or partial header leaves those fields to the question, or to the refusal without a terminal, exactly as if the file had not said them. Nothing about the header refuses a drop, and its lines are still never utterances.
- **The same rule in both processes.** The header is read, and the zone worked out, by one function each, used by the prompt and the daemon, so the prompt asks a question exactly when the drop would need its answer (as `11d` does for the duration with `carried_minutes`).
- **The fixture names no one.** New test files use only the names and words `11d`'s fixture already uses (`Example Weekly Sync-up`, `Alex Morgan`, `Sam Rivera`, placeholder lines). Header variants and a `.docx` with a `docProps/core.xml` are built in the test from the fixture. No line or property from a real export is added.

**Ask First:**
1. ~~Attendees~~ — **answered by Andrei, 2026-10-21: (a).** Attendees stay empty unless `--attendees` is given, and speakers are not attendees; no attendee question is asked.
2. If the recorded fixture's header lines differ from the forms above once read through `docx_text` (for example the title line carries more than the stamp), before writing the header rule.

**Never:** No guessing a person from a display name. No reading the recording stamp in the title as the start. No confirmation line, and no question about a value the file gave. No re-asking the full start when its date and time were read. No document property read from a `.docx` other than `dcterms:created` in `docProps/core.xml` — never `dc:creator`, `cp:lastModifiedBy`, the title or any other — and that one only to work out the start's zone. No write of any kind in `--dry-run`. No question asked by the daemon. No refusal caused by the header alone. No change to the readers' choice, `11b`'s binding, the capture name rule or the sanitization boundary. No real names, spoken words or document properties in a fixture or test.

## Design Notes

The header is read only where `11d`'s turn reader already finds it, so nothing new decides which lines are header and which are speech. The title is the first line and nothing else because a later line is the one most likely to be a date pm-ai failed to read, and a date stored as a meeting's title is worse than a question. The zone matters because it decides binding: `11b` matches a calendar record within 15 minutes of the start, so a start read in the wrong zone mints a second record of the same meeting instead of binding — which is why the closing line always names the offset and where the zone came from, and why `--dry-run` exists for a wrapper that wants to see the outcome first. The calendar step tries every real offset because a record's title and start, both from the calendar, are the strongest evidence of the zone the file was written in; it decides only when exactly one offset matches. The creation-time step is weaker and unmeasured, so it only decides when one offset is left. The recording stamp's time is not used because its zone is not known from the exports seen. Without a terminal the file's values are used, as options are: a pipe has no one to ask. With (a), `11d`'s accepted limit that the prompt asks for attendees before knowing whether a record matches goes away, because the question goes; the same limit for the length stays when `display_timezone` decides at the prompt, because only then does the prompt not consult the daemon.
